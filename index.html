02 · ContextS.E.W.W.W. Prospectus
Section I · The Operating Environment

When the grid fails, every other utility fails with it.

Hurricane Fiona made landfall on Puerto Rico in September 2022, five years after Maria. The entire island lost power. One in three residents lost drinking water. Hundreds of thousands of households were still dark a week later. The Caribbean continues to depend on imported electricity, imported propane, imported fertilizer, and imported food — and the same families pay for the same imports and absorb the same outages every hurricane season. Federal and multilateral capital has been authorized to address this; what is missing is fundable infrastructure that ties the four utilities together at the building level.

03 · Closed-Loop SystemS.E.W.W.W. Prospectus
Section II · System Architecture

One closed loop. Five outputs. Every output is an input somewhere else in the loop.

Sun and wind generate electricity. Electricity charges batteries, runs an atmospheric water generator that produces drinking water, and powers an electrolyzer that creates hydrogen for long-duration backup. Organic waste plus the building's sewer line feeds an anaerobic digester, which produces cooking gas, backup electricity, and fertilizer. The system is designed for 85–90% operational self-sufficiency at the building level.

System schematic shows generation inputs (top), Smart Energy Management System (center), conversion processes (middle row), and primary outputs (bottom). Arrows indicate energy and material flow direction.

Operating principles

Generation redundancyFive generation sources — solar PV, shade-tolerant solar, VAWT wind, biogas generator, hydrogen fuel cell — and three storage types (battery, biogas tank, hydrogen) ensure 24/7 operation across diurnal, seasonal, and disaster scenarios.
Water hierarchyAWG output is reserved 100% for drinking water. Rainwater collection and grey water feed the electrolyzer, so potable water is never consumed for hydrogen production.
Waste-to-resource closureThe anaerobic digester accepts food waste, landscape clippings, and the building's sewer-line fecal stream. Outputs are pipeline-grade biogas (cooking and backup generation) and digestate fertilizer.
Long-duration resilienceHydrogen storage covers 72-plus hours of building demand — sufficient to bridge the typical hurricane recovery window before grid or biogas resumes.
04 · Deployment ModesS.E.W.W.W. Prospectus
Section III · Two Deployment Pathways · One System

New infrastructure or existing building. Same closed loop.

S.E.W.W.W. is engineered for two deployment modes. New infrastructure means the loop is designed in from day one — full integration with build planning, no compromises. Retrofit means the loop ties into the plumbing tree the building already has, using three integration points common across the standard Caribbean residential and commercial plumbing layout. Both modes deploy the same core system; capital structure and timeline differ.

Designed-in from day one

For hotels, hospitals, planned communities, military installations, eco-resorts, government facilities, disaster-relief housing, and rural electrification projects. The closed loop is incorporated into MEP design at the schematic phase. Owner sees lower lifetime utility expense and resilience-rated insurability from occupancy.

Use Cases
Resorts & hotelsPowerTower-tier deployment with full guest-load capacity, on-site drinking water, and digestate fertilizer for landscaping. Caribbean resort developments are the highest-margin SEWWW use case.
HospitalsCritical-load resilience without diesel. FEMA HMGP recently funded a $3.2M PR hospital mitigation project — direct precedent for SEWWW eligibility.
Planned communitiesCommunity-tier deployment per cluster. Replaces grid + utility hookups for entire subdivisions.
Government & militaryOff-grid base power, water, and fuel. Disaster-relief housing manufactured with the loop pre-installed.

Drops into existing plumbing

For existing residential, commercial, and institutional buildings. Three integration points common across the standard Caribbean plumbing layout — cold-water riser, kitchen gas service, and septic chamber. The system retrofits into the tree that's already there. No new plumbing trenching is required.

Integration Points
AWG outputTubería de Agua Fría (A.F.) — PVC SCH-40 cold-water riser. Drinking water flows through existing fixture distribution.
Biogas outputKitchen gas service connection at the stove. Replaces propane delivery; no appliance change.
Digester intakeCámara séptica → pozo filtrante line. Septic effluent intercepted before drain field; produces biogas, electricity, fertilizer.
Existing housing stockMillions of Caribbean homes share this plumbing layout. Retrofit unlocks deployment without new construction.

The DOE Pivot — and what it means

S.E.W.W.W. wins under the new criteria. Rooftop solar alone does not.

On January 6, 2026, DOE notified Congress and OMB that PR-ERF dollars were being redirected from rooftop solar to grid-stabilization and firm-capacity projects. DOE concluded that continuing rooftop-only funding "would exacerbate distribution grid reliability issues."

S.E.W.W.W. is not rooftop solar. It is a closed-loop firm-capacity system with on-site H₂ backup, biogas generation, and multi-source generation redundancy — the exact characteristics DOE is now prioritizing. The pivot creates a clearer funding lane for SEWWW than existed twelve months ago.

Source: DOE Office of Electricity, Puerto Rico Energy Resilience Fund Q&As, January 2026.
