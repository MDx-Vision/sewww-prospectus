Market Need

Caribbean households face recurring infrastructure failure across power, water, fuel, and waste.

The issue is not only the electric grid. When grid service fails, water pumps, refrigeration, sewage systems, and household fuel access are also affected. Families then pay repeatedly for imported energy, bottled water, propane, diesel backup, and recovery costs.

System Model

A single residential infrastructure platform with multiple essential outputs.

The S.E.W.W.W. model integrates solar, wind, atmospheric water generation, anaerobic digestion, battery storage, and hydrogen backup into a coordinated system. Each component is commercially understandable on its own. The value is in the integration.

Retrofit Pathway

The practical advantage is retrofit compatibility, not new-construction dependency.

The Dominican Republic pilot reference includes stamped residential drawings showing standard water, gas, and septic infrastructure. The system is intended to use existing connection points where site conditions and code allow.

Water connection

AWG to cold water system

Atmospheric water generator output can feed storage and pressure systems before distribution through the existing residential cold-water network.

Gas connection

Biogas to cooking use

Biogas can be routed toward cooking or backup generation when the system is engineered, permitted, and safely conditioned for the appliance pathway.

Waste connection

Digester before drain field

Sanitary and organic waste can be intercepted before final disposal, converting a cost and liability stream into a potential energy and fertilizer stream.

Regional Expansion

Puerto Rico can serve as the proof market, with the Dominican Republic as a demonstration bridge.

Puerto Rico offers a strong funding and resilience context. The Dominican Republic provides a site-specific pilot reference. Together, they create a practical path for Caribbean validation and regional delegation visits.

Phase 1

Puerto Rico launch

Use federal and resilience-oriented funding pathways to support an initial deployment in a high-need, high-visibility market.

Phase 2

Use the existing DR documentation and site narrative to support credibility, regional relevance, and investor/funder walkthroughs.

Phase 3

Caribbean replication

Expand to island markets with similar import dependency, grid exposure, water stress, and hurricane recovery challenges.
